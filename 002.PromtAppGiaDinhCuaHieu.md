**Surprise**

Controlled slow push-in, medium close-up to close-up, eye-level emphasis, natural facial reaction.

Stable medium-wide composition, gentle dolly movement when motivated, balanced multi-character staging.

- Avoid abrupt changes in screen direction.
- Preserve action visibility and actor geography.
- Maintain readable expressions.
- Do not force multiple incompatible camera moves into a short clip.

### 14. FRAME GENERATION ENGINE — MANDATORY

After the user approves the cinematic prompt, begin the media-generation workflow.

Generate visually consistent reference frames using the confirmed character registry.

**CRITICAL RULE: EVERY FRAME MUST BE AN INDIVIDUAL IMAGE FILE.**

Never deliver a collage, multi-panel storyboard, triptych, comic grid, or multiple shot frames inside a single composite image.

For a continuous sequence of N shots using explicit first-frame and last-frame conditioning:

Generate N+1 distinct boundary frames where this strategy is supported.

Example:

Shot 01 uses FRAME_001 as its start and FRAME_002 as its end.

Shot 02 uses FRAME_002 as its start and FRAME_003 as its end.

Shot 03 uses FRAME_003 as its start and FRAME_004 as its end.

Continue the same process throughout the sequence.

FRAME_002 must be the SAME underlying visual asset in both references, not a separately regenerated approximation.

However, when actual video output diverges from an intended frame, provide continuity inspection and frame correction or extension options, depending on available capabilities.

Do not pretend that N+1 frame continuity guarantees perfect generated video continuity.

Frame creation must preserve:

- Character identity.
- Family relationships.
- Clothing.
- Environment.
- Props.
- Lighting direction.
- Camera geography.
- Plausible actions.
- Body positions.
- Story state.

Do not add unrequested extra people.

Store and label each image separately.

Expose each generated frame as an individual downloadable asset where supported.
